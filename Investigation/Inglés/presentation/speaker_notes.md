# Presentation notes

Seven-slide English presentation. Development snapshot: 80be128, 14 November 2026. Source citations are also embedded in the editable presentation's notes.

## 1 ARTEMIS HEALTH

Introduce the ARTEMISA team and a developing approach to personal health follow-up during space missions. The proposal combines physical measurements and self-reported wellbeing in an understandable experience. The cover uses an actual NASA Earth photograph and the project's own emblem, without claiming agency endorsement.

## 2 Health follow-up in space missions

Explain that altered gravity, sleep disruption and delayed support create different follow-up needs. The application addresses information organization, not all medical hazards. Orbital microgravity differs from partial surface gravity. Long-term lunar and Martian outcomes remain uncertain. Sources: NASA hazards, Jones et al. 2022 and NTRS 20210019591.

## 3 Proposed application workflow

Walk through profile selection, recording with time and origin, history review and a descriptive explanation. These are specified actions, not completed product endpoints. The proposed comparison excludes future and current-day data and requires compatible valid history. It is an engineering rule, not a medical threshold.

## 4 Four indicators

Explain each measurement and its limits. Sleep and stress are self-reported in this design. Fatigue uses an ordinal scale and should not be interpreted as a ratio. Pulse requires measurement context. No combination of these four fields measures bone density, immune function or a psychological diagnosis.

## 5 Descriptive comparison

All values are synthetic. Fictional profile CM-01 reports 6.85 hours on mission day 15. The median of days 1–14 is 6.13 hours, giving a difference of +0.72 hours. The calculation was produced outside the app for this slide. It illustrates intended descriptive output and does not demonstrate an implemented comparison or clinical benefit. The slide chart remains editable and its numerical input is included in the research evidence.

## 6 Potential mission contribution

Present contributions as hypotheses: consistent records, clearer interpretation and information for authorized review. Each needs a corresponding evaluation. Current persistence requires Supabase connectivity. Complete offline operation, spacecraft networking and clinical effectiveness are not validated.

## 7 Evidence and next steps

State exactly what has been checked: 4,320 synthetic observations, six fictional profiles, two unit tests and 46 data checks. These are software/data results. Product endpoints, remote persistence verification and human evaluation remain pending. Invite collaborators for a defined prototype evaluation and specialist review, rather than implying readiness for flight.
